How Vegas SOR works

One reference team plays every schedule. The ranking measures how often it would finish with at least as many wins as the real team earned.

1. Collect ESPN spreads and final scores

For each completed 2026 FBS regular-season game, we collect the result, site and archived home-team closing spread from ESPN. A home favorite of seven becomes an expected home margin of +7. DraftKings has priority when multiple books are available. The default SOR, VPI and conference rankings use regular-season games only. Conference championships, bowls and playoff games are excluded from both upcoming spreads and completed results. Late regular-season games such as Army–Navy and games against FCS teams count. If a closing spread is unavailable, the result still counts toward the record and the rating fit uses the other available games.

2. Infer team strengths in points

The market quotes a line for the two teams that actually played. To estimate what a common reference team would do, we fit all the lines together. Each game's projected home margin equals the home team's rating minus the visiting team's rating, plus a home-field adjustment. A neutral site gets zero adjustment.

A Kalman filter estimates the ratings one week at a time. Each FBS team starts a week at its previous rating, give or take a weekly drift with a standard deviation of 2 points. Each closing line then moves both teams toward agreement with it, treated as a measurement with a 3.2-point standard error. When no new line involves a team, its rating keeps its last value and only its uncertainty grows. It does not drift toward the national average, so what September's nonconference lines said about two teams that never meet again still counts in November. All lines in the active slate enter that week regardless of kickoff day, and bye weeks still add drift.

Before the first lines, FBS ratings start at zero with a 10-point standard deviation. FCS teams vary only slightly around a shared FCS rating. Home field starts at 2.5 points and is re-estimated along with the ratings. Conference membership is never used, and actual victory margins do not enter the fit. The weekly drift was chosen by predicting each week's closing lines from earlier weeks across the 2021–2025 seasons.

3. Put the reference team on the schedule

The reference rating is the arithmetic mean of the 25 highest-rated FBS teams in that week's market fit. For each opponent we subtract its rating from the reference rating, then add home field, subtract it on the road, or leave the difference unchanged at a neutral site. Every team faces the same benchmark.

p(win) = 1 / (1 + exp(−0.11295 × expected margin))

The conversion was fitted to 3,945 college games with a recorded spread and final score from 2021–25 in The Prediction Tracker. At a projected margin of zero, the reference team wins 50% of the time. A seven-point advantage gives about 69%. These probabilities estimate hypothetical matchups.

4. Count every path to the record

Start with a 100% chance of zero wins. For each game with win chance p, multiply each current win-count probability by 1 − p to stay at the same count, and by p to move up one. After the last game, add the probabilities at or above the team's actual win count. This computes the Poisson-binomial tail exactly under the assumption of independent game outcomes given the ratings.

For a 7–2 team, all paths to seven, eight or nine wins count. A 10% chance earns a higher rank than a 30% chance. Identical schedules and records earn identical scores even if the teams won different games. Teams with zero wins have a 100% chance of matching their record. Numerically equal probabilities share a rank; alphabetical order within a tie is for display.

5. Revisit opponents daily

The daily refresh reads the latest ESPN pregame spread for each scheduled matchup through one active slate. On Sunday after the main Saturday slate, the next official week becomes active. Midweek games stay part of that slate. A pending game from an earlier week enters the filter in its original week. The fit includes each matchup once. When a game finishes, its closing spread replaces its pregame quote and the result enters the record. Upcoming quotes can change both VPI and SOR before a team plays again.

The update timestamp records when collection finished. Coverage counts scheduled, unstarted games in the active week with a spread. Each refresh saves its rankings and the pregame quotes it used. The Update selector on the SOR and VPI pages opens those snapshots. Week labels follow completed results and arrows compare with the preceding result week. Weeks before daily tracking use completed games only. New market information can change the estimated difficulty of a September opponent. The September snapshot keeps its original information boundary. A bye can therefore change a team's current rank. Early rankings are especially sensitive to a single line. ESPN's Week 1 includes the opening weekend games commonly called Week 0. An in-progress week's date identifies the last included game.

What the polls tell you

AP and CFP ranks come from ESPN's dated archives. For a completed week we show the available post-week polls through the following Tuesday, capped at the season data cutoff. An in-progress week may carry the previous release. Each table lists both poll dates. The ranking calculation finishes before the poll data is joined. A blank CFP comparison early in the season means the committee had yet to release a poll.

How well does the model travel?

We replayed the 2021–2025 seasons with the same code. Predicting each week's closing lines from Week 5 onward, using only earlier weeks, the ratings missed the eventual line by 4.7 points (root mean square, 2,768 games). Frozen at the end of the regular season, they predicted conference championship lines within 3.2 points (48 games) and, frozen after championship week, College Football Playoff lines within 3.6 points (31 games). That gap measures some of the information lost in reducing a particular matchup to a pair of team ratings. Lines for 2021–2023 come from ESPN's consensus spread, whose timing ESPN does not document. The research folder holds the backtest.

Changes in personnel, unusual travel, and matchup-specific advantages can all affect a spread. Repricing an early opponent with today's rating can also obscure a genuine change in that team's ability. Small differences between neighboring ranks deserve less weight than large differences in the underlying probabilities.
